# Content guide

## Voice

Gardener & Son sounds like a thoughtful ecological design studio: warm, calm,
grounded, and quietly ambitious. The writing can be poetic, but it must stay
clear.

---

## Copy principles

- Use specific nouns.
- Keep claims believable.
- Prefer ecological clarity over vague inspiration.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Let the reader feel the studio's care and restraint.
- Avoid sounding like AI-generated marketing copy.

---

## Preferred language

ecological gardens; ecological design studio; gardens as living systems;
connecting people to nature through gardens; habitat, soil, water, canopy,
shade, cooling; stewardship; stewards; plants of place; gardens, objects,
plants, stories, systems; beauty under real conditions; indigenous plants of
place.

---

## Language to avoid

professional landscaping solutions; stunning outdoor transformations; your
dream backyard awaits; unlock your garden's potential; world-class;
revolutionary; cutting-edge (unless truly justified); "eco-friendly" as a
standalone claim; "nature-positive" unless tied to a specific claim;
"homeowner" or "landowner" — use **steward**; "native" used alone where the
correct phrase is "indigenous plants of place".

---

## Headings

Sentence case. **Section headings carry a terminal full stop** — this is the
brand-book pattern and the live site follows it.

Good (and matches the live site):

```txt
One practice, held together by ecology.
How we work.
Things worth keeping.
```

Avoid title case:

```txt
Where Gardens Begin
Gardens As Living Systems
```

Brand and product names keep their correct case wherever they appear.

---

## Buttons and navigation

Calm and clear. Button label text **begins with a capital letter** (the live
site follows this — "View Garden Work", "Start Your Journey", "Register Your
Garden").

Good: Start here · View gardens · Visit the studio · Read more · Send enquiry

Avoid: Unlock now · Get started today! · Transform my garden · Let's gooooo

---

## Punctuation

Em dashes suit the brand but should be used sparingly — too many makes the copy
feel mannered. Semicolons only where they genuinely improve rhythm. No
exclamation marks except when quoting a person or preserving warmth in a
direct message.

---

## Capitalisation of names

Use these exact forms — they appear on the live site and must not be
"corrected":

- **Gardener & Son**
- **Ecological Registry**
- **Find My EVC**
- **Find My Ecological Garden**
- **Plants of Place**
- **Heirloom**
- **YIELD**
- **Where Gardens Begin**

Note: it is **Find My**, not "Find Your". Do not change it.

Use lowercase for general descriptions: ecological garden design; garden
designer; studio; registry layer; field notes.

---

## The Acknowledgement of Country

The site carries an Acknowledgement of Country before the footer. Its wording
is **fixed**. Do not edit, shorten, paraphrase, restyle, or relocate it without
explicit instruction from Tyson or Natasha. If a brief seems to require
changing it, stop and ask.

---

## Imprint and attribution

Heirloom and Plants of Place are imprints of Gardener & Son; the site carries a
quiet "An imprint of Gardener & Son." line where appropriate. Keep that quiet
parent-brand presence. Do not overload every section with the full product
stack.

A quiet studio attribution line is acceptable where useful:

```txt
Gardener & Son — ecological design studio, Melbourne
```

---

## Content safety check — before publishing copy

1. Is it true?
2. Is it specific?
3. Does it sound like Gardener & Son?
4. Does it avoid generic landscaping language?
5. Does it help a client, collaborator, council, or investor understand the
   practice more clearly?
