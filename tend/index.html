02 / CHANGE

A garden is a
living system.

What a garden needs in its first year is different from what it needs in its fifth.

Rather than maintaining a garden toward a fixed picture, we watch how it responds to rain, heat, shade, competition, growth and use — intervening where useful and allowing natural processes room where they are working.

03 / TENDING

Care is not
the same as control.

Tending might mean editing vigorous growth, making room around a young plant, replenishing organic matter, adjusting water, observing recruitment, retaining useful fallen material, introducing another layer of planting or simply deciding that nothing needs doing yet.

The aim isn't to keep the garden as it was.
It's to help it become what it can be.
04 / RETURNING

The same eyes,
returning.

Each visit begins with what happened since the last one.

Over time, observations accumulate. We understand which plants are thriving, where water is moving, what is arriving, what is disappearing and how the garden is changing.

That continuity lets us respond to the garden we actually have, rather than repeatedly treating it as a list of maintenance jobs.

05 / RECORD

A garden develops
a memory.

Meaningful observations and changes can be recorded over time — building a history of the garden alongside the garden itself.

For gardens registered with Ecological Registry, that record can also contribute to a longer view of biodiversity, habitat and ecological change.

ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF COUNTRY

At Gardener & Son, we acknowledge the Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung people of the Kulin Nation, the Traditional Custodians of the land on which we live and work. We honour their enduring connection to Country — and the grasslands, woodlands, and wetlands that once flourished here through generations of care and balance. These living systems were not lost to time, but to disconnection. Our work seeks to rekindle that relationship, restoring native ecologies and weaving biodiversity back into the urban fabric — each garden a quiet act of remembrance and renewal.
