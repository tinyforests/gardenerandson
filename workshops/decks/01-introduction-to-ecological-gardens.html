The core idea

What is an ecological garden?

A conventional garden is decorated ground.
An ecological garden is a functioning system.

Plants of this place · layered structure · habitat by design · water held on site
Compare

Two gardens, same block

Conventional

  • Chosen for display
  • Lawn and hedges, little between
  • Watered, fed, sprayed to hold still
  • Rain piped to the street
  • Quiet — few birds, fewer insects

Ecological

  • Chosen for belonging
  • Canopy to ground layer, fully storied
  • Largely runs itself once established
  • Rain slowed and soaked on site
  • Loud — wrens, skinks, native bees

Structure

Anatomy: the four layers

Canopy
Shade, nectar seasons, nesting height — birds, possums, insects
Mid-storey
Dense cover and corridors between gardens — small birds, especially wrens
Ground layer
Grasses, lilies, herbs — the layer the suburbs lost first — butterflies, lizards, native bees
Litter & logs
Soil life and moisture, the slow economy of decay — fungi, beetles, skinks, frogs

Most gardens have a canopy and a lawn — the middle is missing. That is where ecological gardens are won.

Words that matter

Indigenous, native, exotic

Indigenous

From this place — your EVC. Adapted to this soil, this rainfall, and to each other.

Native

Australian, but not necessarily from here. A Western Australian banksia in Hawthorn is a guest.

Exotic

From elsewhere entirely. Not the enemy — just not the engine.

The system's pulse

Water is the quiet organiser

Where rain goes decides what lives.

  • Slow it — litter, ground cover, contour
  • Soak it — living soil holds many times the water of compacted lawn
  • Store it — a shallow dish brings birds within a week
Workbook p. 4

Your first three moves

Small, real, startable this month.

  • Stop spraying
  • Let one corner keep its leaf litter
  • Replace one square metre of lawn with three indigenous grasses
  • Put in a shallow water dish
  • Learn ten plants from your EVC by name
Write yours down — then we will hear one each.
