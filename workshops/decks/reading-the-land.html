Your week ahead
The principle

Observation before design

A good garden answers its site.
So first, you learn to read it.

The commonest and costliest mistake is planting before looking. A wrong plant is a wasted season. One honest week of watching saves years.

What we read

Five things a site tells you

Sun
Where light falls, and for how many hours
Water
Where it arrives, sits, and drains away
Soil
What your ground is actually made of
Aspect

What's there
Existing plants, read as evidence
Reading the sun

Light is not the same
everywhere

Sun moves across your site through the day and swings north through the seasons. A north-facing wall bakes; a south fence stays cool. Measure it in hours, not impressions.

Full sun 6+ hrs · Part shade 3–6 hrs · Full shade under 3 hrs
Reading water & soil

Where water goes,
what soil holds

Water

  • Where it arrives — pipes, slope, next door
  • Where it pools or runs
  • Where it bakes and sheds
  • Where you could slow and soak it

Soil

  • Squeeze test — gritty, sticky, or soft?
  • Settle jar — sand, silt, clay layers
  • Does water sink or sit?
  • Pale and dusty, or dark and alive?
Reading what's already there

Plants are evidence

What grows now tells you what your site is.

  • Weeds and volunteers reveal soil, water, and history
  • What thrives shows you what belongs
  • Remnant indigenous plants are treasure — keep them
Aspect & shelter

One block, many climates

Hot & dry

West-facing walls, paving, the reflected-heat strip. Bakes hard, sheds water.

Cool & damp

Shaded corners, the south side, low ground that holds moisture and stays soft.

Frost pocket

Low spots where cold air settles and lingers white longest on a clear morning.

Find your small climates and you place every plant where it wants to be.

A real site

Reading one garden,
start to finish

We'll walk a real garden together — sun, water, soil, what was already growing — and watch how each observation led to a specific decision.

This is the survey you'll run at home.

Your week ahead

Survey your own site

The method

  • Run the take-home survey booklet
  • Look at morning, midday, afternoon
  • Watch it in sun and in rain
  • Draw your site from above

The point

One honest week stands in for a year of watching. What you record becomes the raw material we design from next week.

The week you spend looking is the cheapest week your garden will ever cost.
