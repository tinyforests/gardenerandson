Healing Country

This garden grows on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country.

We acknowledge the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung people of the Kulin Nation, the Traditional Owners of the land on which this garden grows, and pay our respects to Elders past and present. This land was never ceded.

Every locally indigenous plant returned to a home garden is a small act of healing Country — habitat restored, soil brought back to life, and a living thread reconnected between one front yard and the wider landscape it belongs to.

The place as we found it

A small garden with a generous spirit.

Lynette's garden already holds the memory of a home lived in and cared for over many years. The curved brick path, the brick edging, the grapevine over the porch, the roses, hibiscus, birdbath and shaded rear corners all give the garden its quiet character.

The design does not seek to erase this history. It gently strengthens it — with new soil, mulch, and a resilient planting layer that feels natural, flowering, bird-friendly and deeply connected to the house.

Pocket 01 · The front strip

Lynette's flowering habitat garden.

The most public part of the garden — experienced by neighbours, children and everyone walking past along the white fence. Your roses stay, woven into the new planting as part of the garden's lived history: familiar, flowering and generous.

Around them, grasses, correas, banksias, everlasting daisies, goodenia, scaevola, bulbine, chocolate lily and native violet bring movement, colour and habitat through every season.

The year in flower

Something in bloom, every season.

Spring

Wattle gold, chocolate lily, everlastings, bluebells, austral indigo and the garden at full voice.

Summer

Tussock grasses in plume, kangaroo paw, fan flowers, native flax and daisies carrying the heat.

Autumn

The grapevine turns; white correa and banksia candles begin as the garden softens.

Winter

Correa bells, hardenbergia, running postman and waxflower — quiet colour and nectar for the birds.

A continuous nectar calendar means the garden never falls silent — honeyeaters, butterflies and native bees have a reason to visit in every month of the year. A full flowering calendar and plant list come with this proposal as A3 prints.

The Ecological Registry

A garden that counts.

Once planted, Lynette's garden can be enrolled in the Ecological Registry — documented, scored and verified as a Registered Ecological Garden, with its own Registry ID and a record that grows with the garden.

Documented

The planting, soil and habitat features are recorded — a living archive of the garden from day one.

Verified

Scored across five ecological pillars, from biodiversity structure to habitat complexity and connectivity.

Connected

One garden joins a growing network of verified habitat gardens across Melbourne's east — each a stepping stone for birds, insects and life moving through the suburbs.

Tend · Garden stewardship

Care that continues.

A new garden is most vulnerable in its first season. Tend is our stewardship subscription — seasonal visits from the people who designed and planted the garden, keeping it establishing, flowering and healthy as it matures.

Six weeks in — on us

Your first Tend visit, six weeks after planting, is complimentary: establishment check, watering review, mulch top-up and any early adjustments.

Seasonal care

Pruning, cutting back grasses, plant health and gentle editing — timed to the garden's rhythm, not a mowing schedule.

A garden that improves

Each visit builds the garden's Registry record, so its ecological story is kept as carefully as the garden itself.

Next steps

A garden renewed, slowly.

From here: we confirm the palette together, prepare the soil and mulch layer, and plant into the cooler months so everything settles in before summer. The roses, the vine, the birdbath — all stay. Everything new grows around what you already love.
