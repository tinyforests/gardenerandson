The idea

Sown, not arranged.

The old brick edging, the courtyard and the stepping pads all stay — they already have the age the planting will take years to earn. The Lamb's Ear and Ajuga already thriving here are lifted, divided and replanted into the new scheme.

Through the middle, a softly wandering crushed-granite path gives the garden one clear line of movement — informal enough to feel discovered rather than imposed, and narrow enough that the planting can lean into it and soften its edges.

The planting

Four threads, one community.

Structure

Six grasses

The grasses do the structural work — the vertical line, the movement, and the shape that holds once everything else has gone over. Feather Reed Grass keeps the upright note, Tufted Hair Grass throws clouds of bronze and gold that glow when backlit, and Red Buttons scatters soft burgundy heads through the beds.

  • Feather Reed Grass 'Karl Foerster'
  • Tufted Hair Grass
  • Red Buttons
  • Mexican Feather Grass
  • Common Tussock-Grass
  • Longhair Plume Grass
Colour

Violet-blue and deep burgundy

Four salvias carry colour for most of the year. Russian Sage and Blue Sea Holly add smoky haze and steel-blue thimbles, Tall Verbena floats above on see-through stems, and 'Black Knight' Scabiosa brings the darkest note in the garden.

  • Tall Verbena
  • Bronze Fennel
  • Blue Sea Holly
  • Lesser Knotweed
  • Salvia 'Waverly'
  • Salvia 'Mystic Spires'
  • Salvia 'African Skies'
The silver thread

Lifted and scented

A silver thread of Wormwood, Bush Germander, Lamb's Ear and Bronze Fennel lifts the darker flowers and scents the air when brushed. It is what keeps the burgundy from going heavy.

The ground layer

Closing the soil

At ground level, Native Violet and Alpine Pratia knit the shady pockets with Ajuga, while Seaside Daisy softens the edges and the brick. A closed ground layer means less weeding, not more.

  • Seaside Daisy
  • Alpine Pratia
  • Native Violet
  • Carpet Bugle
Plants of this place

An indigenous thread.

Running through the meadow is a thread of plants indigenous to this place — tussock and plume grasses, chocolate lily and magenta stork's-bill — so the garden feeds the insects and birds that already belong here.

Acknowledgement of Country

At Gardener & Son, we acknowledge the Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung people of the Kulin Nation, the Traditional Custodians of the land on which we live and work. We honour their enduring connection to Country — and the grasslands, woodlands, and wetlands that once flourished here through generations of care and balance. These living systems were not lost to time, but to disconnection. Our work seeks to rekindle that relationship, restoring native ecologies and weaving biodiversity back into the urban fabric — each garden a quiet act of remembrance and renewal.
